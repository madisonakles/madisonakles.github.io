🧠 Research Gaps & Literature Context

1. Expressive vs. Strategic Sharing:
Current literature (e.g., Van Bavel & Pereira, 2018) suggests users share misinformation to "signal" group loyalty ("Expressive"). However, this simulation isolates "Strategic" sharing—where the user knows the info might be false but shares it to achieve a professional utility (winning the election).

2. The "Accuracy Nudge" Limitation:
While Pennycook & Rand (2021) focus on "Accuracy Nudges" to reduce sharing in the general public, this 'War Room' simulation investigates Strategic Misinformation in professional settings. It asks: Does the pressure to 'win' in a high-stakes role override the cognitive capacity for verification?

📝 Future Direction: Counter-Attitudinal Role-Playing
A critical next step for this simulation is assigning users to the opposing political party (e.g., a Democrat playing as a Republican Manager).

This tests Cognitive Dissonance: Does the "Professional Duty" to the role override their personal moral alignment? Can we induce someone to spread misinformation against their own tribe purely through gamified incentives?
